# PRD: Expense Log — Round 2 (Duplicate Detection, Last-Entry Display, Mobile Navigation)

## Problem Statement

Three gaps have shown up in day-to-day use of the expense log:

1. **Duplicate expenses slip in.** Users sometimes enter the same expense twice — either by accident in one sitting, or because one household member logs a purchase that another already logged a few days earlier. The amounts are often not byte-identical (a tip added, a rounding difference, $100.00 vs $100.50) and the dates are often a day or two apart, so the duplicate is hard to spot in the list. Nothing in the app warns the user before the second copy is saved, and duplicates silently inflate totals and summaries.
2. **No clear confirmation of what was just saved.** After a save, the page redirects back to the list with a generic "Expense added." flash. If the expense's date falls outside the current list window, or the list is long, the user cannot easily see *what* they just entered to double-check it.
3. **The navigation bar is cramped on phones.** The header shows five main-page links (Expenses, Categories, Tags, Summary, Recurring) plus Profile, Sign out, and a welcome message as a row of buttons. On a mobile browser this row overflows or wraps awkwardly, making navigation clumsy on the device where quick entry matters most.

## Solution

When this round is complete:

- **Duplicate warning on entry.** When a user submits a new expense through the entry form, the app checks for existing expenses with a similar amount (within the larger of $1.00 or 1% of the larger of the two amounts, inclusive — identical amounts count) and a date within ±4 days (inclusive). If any are found, instead of saving, the app shows a dedicated "Possible duplicates" page listing the pending expense at the top and every potential duplicate below it (closest date first). The user chooses **Save** (save anyway), **Cancel** (back to the form with all values preserved), or **Discard** (back to a cleared form). Duplicate detection runs only for human-entered new expenses — never for edits, deletes, or recurring-generated rows. If the submission also introduces new categories or tags, the duplicates page comes first; the existing "create new category/tags?" confirmation follows only if the user chooses Save.
- **Last-entry alert.** After an expense is successfully created or edited, the expenses page shows a prominent DaisyUI info alert (outlined) between the entry form and the filter bar/list, containing the saved expense's date, description, category, tags, formatted amount, and an Edit link. The existing green "Expense added." / "Expense updated." flash remains. The alert is one-shot: it disappears on the next navigation, including Filter, Clear filters, or a reload.
- **Responsive navigation.** On viewports narrower than Tailwind's `md` breakpoint (768px), the five main-page links plus Profile collapse into a single dropdown button labelled with the current section name and a down-arrow; Welcome text and the Sign out button stay visible beside it. On `md` and wider, the existing link row is shown unchanged except that the current page's link is visually highlighted. The dropdown works without JavaScript.

## User Stories

### Duplicate detection — detection rules

1. As a signed-in user entering a new expense, I want the app to check whether a similar expense already exists before saving, so that I don't accidentally record the same spend twice.
2. As a signed-in user, I want an existing expense to count as a potential duplicate when its amount is within the larger of $1.00 or 1% of the larger of the two amounts (inclusive), so that small differences like tips or rounding are still caught.
3. As a signed-in user, I want an existing expense with an *identical* amount to count as a potential duplicate (subject to the date rule), so that exact re-entries are caught.
4. As a signed-in user, I want an existing expense to count as a potential duplicate only when its date is within 4 calendar days of the new expense's date, in either direction, inclusive, so that unrelated similar purchases weeks apart are not flagged.
5. As a signed-in user, I want both the amount rule and the date rule to have to hold for a match, so that I am only warned about genuinely suspicious entries.
6. As a signed-in user, I want description, category, and tags to be ignored when deciding whether something is a potential duplicate, so that duplicates entered with different wording or categorization are still caught.
7. As a signed-in user, I want my new entry compared against every existing expense — those entered by any signed-in user and those generated by recurring templates — so that I catch a bill the cron already logged or a purchase another household member already entered.
8. As a signed-in user, when an expense is $50.00 and another is $51.00, I want them flagged (difference $1.00 ≤ $1.00 floor), so that the absolute floor protects small amounts.
9. As a signed-in user, when an expense is $50.00 and another is $51.01, I want them *not* flagged (difference $1.01 > max($1.00, 1% of $51.01 ≈ $0.51)), so that the threshold is predictable.
10. As a signed-in user, when an expense is $1,000.00 and another is $1,010.00, I want them flagged (difference $10.00 ≤ 1% of $1,010.00 = $10.10), so that the percentage rule scales for large amounts.
11. As a signed-in user, when a new expense is dated the 10th, I want expenses dated the 6th through the 14th (inclusive) to be candidates and the 5th or 15th not to be, so that the window is unambiguous — including across month and year boundaries.

### Duplicate detection — the duplicates page

12. As a signed-in user, when one or more potential duplicates exist, I want to be taken to a separate "Possible duplicates" page instead of the expense being saved, so that I can decide before anything is written.
13. As a signed-in user, I want the pending expense (date, description, category, tags, formatted amount) shown at the top of the duplicates page, so that I can compare it side-by-side with the existing ones.
14. As a signed-in user, when the pending expense names a category or tags that do not exist yet, I want them shown as typed on the duplicates page (they are not created yet), so that I see exactly what I entered.
15. As a signed-in user, I want every potential duplicate listed — not just the first — so that I can see all the candidates at once.
16. As a signed-in user, I want each potential duplicate shown the same way as a row in the main expense list (date, description, category, tags, formatted amount, recurring badge for generated rows), but without an Edit button, so that the page is familiar and doesn't lead me out of the decision.
17. As a signed-in user, I want potential duplicates ordered by closest date first, then by smallest amount difference, so that the most likely duplicate is at the top.
18. As a signed-in user, I want a **Save** action on the duplicates page that saves my expense anyway, so that I can record legitimately similar expenses (e.g., two identical coffees on consecutive days).
19. As a signed-in user, I want a **Cancel** action that returns me to the entry form with every value (description, amount, date, category text, selected tag chips, new-tag text) preserved, so that I can adjust my entry.
20. As a signed-in user, I want a **Discard** action that returns me to a cleared entry form, so that I can abandon a true duplicate in one click.
21. As a signed-in user, after Discard I want a brief flash message confirming that nothing was saved, so that I am confident the duplicate was not recorded.
22. As a signed-in user, after Save I want the normal post-save experience (success flash, last-entry alert, cleared form), so that saving through the duplicates page feels the same as a direct save.
23. As a signed-in user with JavaScript disabled, I want the duplicates page and all three actions to work as plain form posts, so that the feature degrades gracefully.

### Duplicate detection — interaction with new category/tag confirmation

24. As a signed-in user whose submission both introduces a new category or tags and has potential duplicates, I want to see the duplicates page first, so that I don't confirm creating new category/tags for an expense I then discard.
25. As a signed-in user, after choosing Save on the duplicates page for a submission that introduces new category/tags, I want to be taken to the existing "create new items?" confirmation page, so that new items are still explicitly confirmed.
26. As a signed-in user, when I confirm on the new-items page after approving duplicates, I want the expense saved without being shown the duplicates page a second time (unless new duplicates have appeared), so that I am not asked the same question twice.
27. As a signed-in user, if I cancel on the new-items confirmation page after having approved duplicates, I want to return to the entry form with values preserved and my duplicate approval forgotten, so that a later resubmission is checked afresh.
28. As a signed-in user whose submission introduces new items but has no potential duplicates, I want the existing new-items confirmation flow unchanged, so that nothing regresses.

### Duplicate detection — scope boundaries

29. As a signed-in user editing an existing expense, I want no duplicate check, so that edits are never blocked by a warning.
30. As a signed-in user deleting an expense, I want no duplicate check, so that deletes are never blocked.
31. As a signed-in user with recurring templates, I want recurring-generated expenses to be inserted by the cron without any duplicate check, so that scheduled entries always go through unattended.
32. As a signed-in user creating or editing a recurring *template*, I want no duplicate check, so that template management is unaffected.

### Duplicate detection — re-check, races, and failures

33. As a signed-in user, when I choose Save on the duplicates page, I want the server to re-run the duplicate check and, if any potential duplicate now exists that was not on the page I approved, show me the duplicates page again with the updated list, so that I never unknowingly approve a duplicate I wasn't shown.
34. As a signed-in user, if a potential duplicate I was shown has since been deleted, I want Save to proceed without re-prompting, so that removals don't cause pointless re-confirmation.
35. As a signed-in user, when I confirm the new-items page after approving duplicates, I want the same re-check applied, so that the guarantee holds across the whole flow.
36. As a signed-in user, if the duplicate lookup fails because of a database error, I want the expense *not* saved and to be returned to the form with all values preserved and the message "Failed to save expense. Please try again.", so that a failure never silently saves a possible duplicate.
37. As a signed-in user, when I choose Save, I want every field fully re-validated on the server (hidden fields are not trusted), and any validation failure to return me to the form with values preserved and field errors shown, so that tampered or stale values cannot be saved.
38. As a signed-in user, if a selected tag was deleted between submit and Save, I want the existing recoverable "One or more selected tags no longer exist." error, so that behaviour matches the entry form.
39. As a signed-in user, if my Save submission is retried by the resilient-submit layer after the server already committed it, I want no second expense created and the same success result shown, so that retries never double-book.
40. As a signed-in user, I want a Cancel or Discard action never to consume my submission's idempotency key, so that a later genuine save from the same form still works.
41. As a signed-in user, I want a validation error on the entry form to be reported before any duplicate check, so that I fix typos first and only see the duplicates page for valid entries.
42. As a signed-in user, I want the resilient-submit layer never to retry the duplicates page response (it is a deliberately server-rendered page), so that I am not bounced around by retries.

### Last-entry alert

43. As a signed-in user, after I successfully create an expense (directly, via the new-items confirmation, or via Save on the duplicates page), I want a prominent info alert showing what I just saved, so that I can verify my entry at a glance.
44. As a signed-in user, after I successfully save an edit to an expense, I want the same alert showing the updated expense, so that I can verify the change.
45. As a signed-in user, I want the alert placed between the entry form and the filter bar/expense list, styled as a DaisyUI outlined info alert, so that it is visually distinct from both.
46. As a signed-in user, I want the alert to show the expense's date, description, category, tags, and formatted amount, so that I see everything I entered.
47. As a signed-in user, I want the alert to include an Edit link for that expense, so that I can fix a mistake immediately.
48. As a signed-in user, I want the alert shown even when the saved expense's date falls outside the currently displayed list window, so that I can always see what I entered.
49. As a signed-in user, I want the existing green success flash ("Expense added." / "Expense updated.") to remain alongside the alert, so that the familiar confirmation is unchanged.
50. As a signed-in user, I want the alert removed on my next operation — Filter, Clear filters, or any other navigation, including a browser reload — with no automatic timed dismissal, so that it never lingers or vanishes while I'm reading it.
51. As a signed-in user, if the saved expense was deleted (e.g., by another user) before the list page renders, I want no alert and no error, so that the page still loads normally.
52. As a signed-in user, if loading the saved expense for the alert fails unexpectedly, I want the page to render normally without the alert, so that a cosmetic feature never breaks the list.
53. As a signed-in user, if my save was committed but the response was lost and the resilient-submit retry is answered from the idempotency ledger, I want the alert to still appear for the expense that was saved, so that retries look identical to first-time success.
54. As a signed-in user, I want no alert after a validation error, a cancel, a discard, a delete, or any failed save, so that the alert always means "this was just saved".
55. As a signed-in user, I want user-entered values in the alert (description, category, tags) HTML-escaped, so that the alert is safe.

### Responsive navigation

56. As a signed-in user on a viewport narrower than 768px, I want the Expenses, Categories, Tags, Summary, Recurring, and Profile links replaced by a single dropdown button, so that the header fits on my phone.
57. As a signed-in user on mobile, I want the dropdown button labelled with the name of the section I'm in, followed by a down-arrow, so that I know where I am and that it opens a menu.
58. As a signed-in user on mobile, I want tapping the button to open a list of the main pages plus Profile, and tapping an item to navigate there, so that I can switch pages in two taps.
59. As a signed-in user on mobile, I want the "Welcome, name!" text and the Sign out button to remain visible outside the dropdown, so that signing out stays one tap away.
60. As a signed-in user on a viewport 768px or wider, I want the existing row of links (Expenses, Categories, Tags, Summary, Recurring, Profile, Sign out) and the Welcome text unchanged, so that the desktop experience is not disrupted.
61. As a signed-in user on either layout, I want the link/item for my current section visually highlighted and marked as the current page for assistive technology, so that I can orient myself.
62. As a signed-in user on a page that belongs to a main section but isn't its landing page (edit expense, duplicates page, new-items confirmation, edit/delete recurring, category/tag merge confirmation), I want the button labelled with the owning section (e.g., "Expenses", "Recurring", "Categories", "Tags"), so that the label still makes sense.
63. As a signed-in user on the Profile page (or its sub-pages), I want the button labelled "Profile", so that the label matches the dropdown item.
64. As a signed-in user on any other page with no owning section, I want the button labelled "Menu", so that it is never blank or misleading.
65. As a signed-in user with JavaScript disabled, I want the dropdown to open and its links to work, so that mobile navigation never depends on scripts.
66. As a signed-in user using a keyboard or screen reader, I want the dropdown button focusable and the menu items reachable and announced as links, so that navigation is accessible.
67. As an unauthenticated visitor, I want the navigation unchanged (only the Sign in button), so that this change does not affect public pages.

## Implementation Decisions

### Duplicate matching rules (stable interface, tunable values)

- A *potential duplicate* of a pending expense is any existing expense row where **both**:
  - `|pendingAmountCents − existingAmountCents| ≤ tolerance`, where `tolerance = max(100, round-half-up(1% × max(pendingAmountCents, existingAmountCents)))` (cents, inclusive; identical amounts always match).
  - `|pendingDate − existingDate| ≤ 4` calendar days (inclusive), computed as calendar-day differences on `YYYY-MM-DD` values (no timezone conversion; month/year boundaries and leap years handled correctly).
- Description, category, tags, author, and recurring provenance are not inputs to matching.
- The tolerance values ($1.00 floor, 1%, 4 days) are named code constants owned by the matcher module. The user expects matching rules to change in future (thresholds, or adding description/category similarity), so callers depend only on the matcher's interface, never on the constants or the formula.
- Ordering of matches: ascending absolute day difference, then ascending absolute amount difference, then date descending, then description ascending (case-insensitive) as a final deterministic tiebreak.
- All candidates are shown; there is no cap (the ±4-day window keeps the set small).

### Entry pipeline

- The three create entry points — initial entry POST, duplicates-page Save, and new-items confirmation POST — share a single decision function (the *expense entry pipeline*) so the ordering is defined in one place:
  1. Parse and validate fields (existing validators); on failure → redirect to the form with field errors and values preserved.
  2. Validate tag input against existing tags (existing contract: unknown/stale `tagId` → recoverable global error).
  3. Duplicate check (skipped only per the approval rule below). DB failure → fail closed: redirect to the form with "Failed to save expense. Please try again." and values preserved.
  4. New-category/new-tag detection; if anything is new → render the existing new-items confirmation page.
  5. Commit through the idempotency ledger.
- **Approval rule:** a submission may carry the set of expense ids the user was shown and approved on the duplicates page. At step 3 the pipeline recomputes matches; if every current match id is in the approved set, the check passes; if any current match is not in the approved set, the duplicates page is rendered again with the fresh list (and the approved set replaced by the new one on that render). Approved ids that no longer match (e.g., deleted) are ignored.
- The approved-id set is round-tripped as hidden fields on the duplicates page and carried forward onto the new-items confirmation page when that page follows a duplicates Save. Cancel on either page drops it, so a later resubmission is checked afresh.
- Tampering with the approved-id set can at most suppress a duplicate warning for the tamperer's own submission; this is accepted. Protection on the duplicates page is full server-side re-validation on Save, matching the existing new-items confirmation page (no HMAC).
- Edit, delete, recurring template create/edit, and the recurring materializer do not call the pipeline's duplicate step. The recurring materializer is untouched.

### Duplicates page and route

- New authenticated POST route under the expenses section (alongside the existing create-confirmation route), protected by the existing signed-in middleware and secure-headers configuration used by the expenses routes.
- The page renders: a heading, the pending expense summary (category and tags shown as typed; new names are not yet created), the list of potential duplicates in the list-row format (including recurring badge, no Edit button), and a form carrying the pending values, `submissionKey`, and approved-id set as hidden fields, with three submit buttons distinguished by an `action` field: `save`, `cancel`, `discard`.
- `save` → re-enter the pipeline with the approved-id set.
- `cancel` → redirect to the expenses page via the existing form-state flash with all values preserved and no errors (same mechanism as the existing confirmation cancel).
- `discard` → redirect to the expenses page with a cleared form and a flash message stating the expense was not saved (wording: "Expense not saved.").
- Neither `cancel` nor `discard` writes to the idempotency ledger. The `submissionKey` is preserved across the duplicates page and any following new-items confirmation so the final commit remains idempotent.
- The page is a deliberately server-rendered 200 response; the resilient-submit client must treat it like the existing confirmation page (no retry). No change to retry classification is expected, but this must be verified.

### Data access

- New query `findPotentialDuplicates(db, { amountCents, date })` returning `Result<ExpenseRow[], Error>` in the same row shape as the list (including category name, tag names, recurring id). It prefilters in SQL by date range (`date ± 4 days`) and a conservative amount range that is a superset of the tolerance, then applies the matcher for the exact decision and ordering. It uses the existing retrying DB-access helpers and logging discipline (no descriptions/amounts in logs).
- New single-expense read for the alert reuses the existing get-by-id access function (or a thin variant returning the list-row shape with category and tag names).
- **No schema changes.** No migration is required.

### Last-entry alert

- On every successful create commit (direct, new-items confirm, duplicates Save) and every successful edit commit (direct and edit-with-new-items confirm), the handler sets a one-shot *last-entry* cookie containing only the saved expense's id, then performs the normal post-redirect-get to the expenses page.
- The idempotency outcome is extended with an optional `lastEntryExpenseId`. To make this possible for creates, the expense id is generated in the handler before the commit and passed into the create functions, so the stored outcome is known up front. On replay from the ledger, the handler sets the same last-entry cookie from the stored outcome. Outcomes stored before this change (no field) replay without an alert. Outcome parsing must accept both shapes.
- The expenses GET handler reads-and-clears the cookie, loads that expense fresh from the DB, and renders the alert between the entry form and the filter bar. Missing expense or read failure → no alert, page renders normally (failure logged per existing discipline).
- Because the cookie is consumed on the first render, any subsequent navigation (Filter and Clear filters are plain GETs; reload) shows no alert. No client-side dismissal logic or timer.
- The alert markup is a DaisyUI `alert alert-info alert-outline` with `role="alert"`, containing a "Last saved" label, date, description, category, tags (comma-separated, empty if none), formatted amount (existing money formatter), and an Edit link to that expense's edit page. All values are JSX-escaped.
- The existing success flash ("Expense added." / "Expense updated.") is unchanged and still shown at the top of the layout.
- The edit flow already redirects to the expenses page on success; that stays.

### Responsive navigation

- The layout's signed-in header is rendered by a nav renderer that takes the current section, which is resolved from the request path by a pure resolver.
- Sections: `expenses`, `categories`, `tags`, `summary`, `recurring`, `profile`, `none`. Labels: "Expenses", "Categories", "Tags", "Summary", "Recurring", "Profile", and "Menu" for `none`.
- Resolver mapping by path prefix: the expenses list and every path under the expenses section (edit, delete confirmation, create confirmation, duplicates page) → `expenses`; categories paths (including rename/merge confirmation) → `categories`; tags paths → `tags`; summary → `summary`; recurring paths (create, edit, delete, confirmation) → `recurring`; profile paths → `profile`; anything else (root, 404, auth pages) → `none`. Prefix matching must not mis-assign look-alike paths (match on path-segment boundaries).
- Below `md` (768px): a DaisyUI focus-based dropdown (no JavaScript required) whose trigger button shows the current section label and a down-arrow icon; its menu lists Expenses, Categories, Tags, Summary, Recurring, Profile in that order. Welcome text and the Sign out form remain visible outside the dropdown. The desktop link row is hidden at this width.
- At `md` and above: the existing link row (including Profile and Sign out) and Welcome text are shown; the dropdown is hidden.
- The current section's link (desktop) and menu item (mobile) receive an active visual style and `aria-current="page"`.
- Existing `data-testid` values on desktop links are retained; mobile elements get distinct test ids (e.g., a nav-dropdown trigger id and per-item ids) so both layouts are independently testable.
- Unauthenticated header (Sign in button only) is unchanged.
- Responsiveness is implemented purely with Tailwind responsive utilities; no user-agent sniffing.

## Module Design

### 1. Duplicate Matcher

- **Name**: Duplicate matcher
- **Responsibility**: Owns the definition of "potential duplicate" — tolerance constants, the amount/date rules, and result ordering.
- **Interface**: Given a pending `{ amountCents, date }` and a list of candidate expense rows, returns the subset that are potential duplicates, ordered by closest date, then smallest amount difference, then deterministic tiebreaks. Also exposes the SQL prefilter bounds (date range and a conservative amount range) for the lookup to use, so the lookup never re-derives the formula. Pure; no failure modes beyond typed input. The rules/constants are expected to change; the function signatures are stable.
- **Tested**: Yes (unit) — boundary cases at exactly $1.00 / $1.01, 1% vs floor crossover, identical amounts, ±4 vs ±5 days, month/year/leap-year boundaries, rounding of the 1% term, ordering and tiebreaks.

### 2. Duplicate Lookup

- **Name**: Duplicate lookup (data access)
- **Responsibility**: Fetches candidate expense rows from the database and returns the matcher-filtered, ordered potential duplicates.
- **Interface**: `findPotentialDuplicates(db, { amountCents, date })` → `Result<ExpenseRow[], Error>` in list-row shape. Errors are DB/transport failures only.
- **Tested**: Yes (unit, against the test DB) — includes manual and recurring-generated rows, excludes out-of-window rows, returns correct row shape with category and tags, returns an error under forced DB failure.

### 3. Expense Entry Pipeline

- **Name**: Expense entry pipeline
- **Responsibility**: Single source of truth for the create-flow decision order: validate → tag checks → duplicate check (honouring the approved-id set) → new-items detection → ready to commit.
- **Interface**: Input: raw form values (including `submissionKey` and optional approved-id set). Output: a discriminated result — `validation-error` (field errors + preserved values), `load-error` (fail-closed DB failure), `duplicates` (pending values + ordered matches + new approved-id set), `confirm-new-items` (data for the existing confirmation page, carrying the approved-id set), or `ready-to-commit` (resolved category/tags + values). Handlers map each variant to a response; the commit itself stays in the handlers via the idempotency ledger.
- **Tested**: Yes (unit, against the test DB) — each variant, the ordering (validation before duplicates; duplicates before new items), approval suppression, re-prompt when a new match appears, approved-but-deleted ids ignored, fail-closed on lookup error.

### 4. Duplicates Page and Handler

- **Name**: Duplicates page + POST handler
- **Responsibility**: Renders the possible-duplicates page and handles its Save / Cancel / Discard actions.
- **Interface**: Renderer takes pending values, resolved display names, the ordered matches, the approved-id set, and the `submissionKey`. The handler reads `action`; `save` delegates to the pipeline; `cancel` and `discard` redirect as specified. Failure modes are the pipeline's.
- **Tested**: Yes (E2E).

### 5. Last-Entry Flash

- **Name**: Last-entry flash
- **Responsibility**: Carries "which expense was just saved" from a successful commit (or ledger replay) to the next expenses-page render, and renders the alert.
- **Interface**: Set (context, expenseId), read-and-clear (context) → expenseId or nothing; alert renderer takes an expense list row. The idempotency outcome type gains an optional `lastEntryExpenseId`, and outcome parsing accepts outcomes with or without it. A missing expense or read failure yields no alert.
- **Tested**: Yes — unit for outcome parsing (old and new shapes) and replay carrying the id; E2E for visible behaviour.

### 6. Main Navigation

- **Name**: Main nav (section resolver + renderer)
- **Responsibility**: Determines the current section from the request path and renders the responsive signed-in navigation.
- **Interface**: Resolver: path → section (`expenses | categories | tags | summary | recurring | profile | none`), pure. Renderer: current section (plus user display name) → header markup with desktop links and mobile dropdown. No failure modes.
- **Tested**: Yes — unit for the resolver (every section, sub-pages, look-alike prefixes, unknown paths → `none`); E2E for both viewports.

## Testing Decisions

- **What makes a good test here:** assert externally observable behaviour — which rows are flagged and in what order, which page the user lands on, which values are preserved, what the alert shows, which nav elements are visible at a given viewport — not internal helper calls or markup structure beyond stable test ids.
- **Unit tests (bun):**
  - Duplicate matcher: full boundary table (see Module 1).
  - Nav section resolver: mapping table including sub-pages and look-alike paths.
  - Duplicate lookup and entry pipeline against the test DB helper, including forced DB-error paths.
  - Idempotency outcome parsing with and without `lastEntryExpenseId`, and replay reproducing the id.
- **E2E tests (Playwright):**
  - Duplicate flow: no-duplicate entry saves directly; duplicate entry shows page with pending expense on top and all matches ordered closest-date-first; recurring-generated row is offered as a match; Save saves and shows success + alert; Cancel returns with all values (including tag chips and new-tag text) preserved; Discard returns to a cleared form with the "not saved" flash; duplicates + new category/tags shows duplicates first then new-items confirmation, then saves; cancel from new-items after approval forgets approval; re-prompt when a new matching expense is inserted between page render and Save; edit never shows the duplicates page; no-JS flow works; forced DB error fails closed with values preserved.
  - Last-entry alert: appears after create, after create via each confirmation path, and after edit; positioned between form and filter bar; shows all fields and an Edit link that works; shown when the date is outside the list window; gone after Filter, Clear filters, and reload; absent after validation error, cancel, discard, and delete; resilient-submit replay still shows it.
  - Navigation: at a mobile viewport, links are hidden and the dropdown shows the current section label with arrow; opening it lists the six items and navigating works; Welcome text and Sign out are visible; section labels correct on sub-pages ("Expenses" on edit/duplicates pages, "Recurring" on recurring edit, "Profile" on profile); works with JS disabled. At a desktop viewport, the link row is visible, the dropdown is hidden, and the active link is highlighted with `aria-current`.
- **Prior art:** handler-level unit tests such as the expense-confirm and recurring-confirm handler specs and the test-DB helper; expense-access and submission-idempotency specs for data-access and ledger tests; E2E specs for the new-items confirmation flow (entry new-tag confirmation, inline category creation), no-JS fallback, resilient-submit happy path/retry, and the existing `withForcedDbError` support for DB-failure injection.

## Out of Scope

- Duplicate detection on expense edits, deletes, recurring template create/edit, or recurring materialization (cron or dev trigger).
- Matching on description, category, tags, or author; fuzzy text similarity of any kind.
- User-configurable or environment-configurable tolerances; any settings UI.
- A standalone "find all duplicates in my history" report or bulk duplicate cleanup.
- Merging, deleting, or editing an existing expense from the duplicates page (rows have no Edit/Delete buttons there).
- Marking a pair of expenses as "not duplicates" so they are never flagged again.
- HMAC signing of the duplicates page or the existing new-items confirmation page.
- Timed or manual (close button) dismissal of the last-entry alert; persisting it across reloads.
- Last-entry alert for category, tag, or recurring template saves, or for expense deletes.
- Changing or removing the existing green success flash.
